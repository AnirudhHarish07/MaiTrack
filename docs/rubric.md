CRO / Vendor Evaluation Rubric — ADA, PK & Toxicology (China / India)
How to use this workbook
1. Go to the 'Scorecard' tab. Each row is one CRO/vendor.
2. For every sub-criterion, enter a score using the tier bands listed below (yellow cells = your input).
3. Category subtotals, the grand total (/100), and the Tier 1/2/3/Excluded classification calculate automatically (green cells = formulas — do not overwrite).
4. 'Hard Gate Passed?' must be marked N if the vendor lacks valid GLP status, has an unresolved major regulatory finding, or refuses a standard CDA — this auto-excludes them regardless of score.
5. 'Location Tier' for Accessibility is a dropdown — selecting 'Outside Asia' auto-excludes the vendor regardless of score.

How 'Insufficient Data' and 'N/A' are handled
• A sub-criterion cell showing "Insufficient Data" means no verifiable public source was found for that specific item — it is NOT the same as a score of 0, and is excluded from that category's subtotal rather than counted as a failing score.
• If every sub-criterion in a category is "Insufficient Data", that category's Subtotal shows "N/A" instead of 0, so a genuine gap in public information is never visually confused with a genuinely poor score.
• The Grand Total sums only the categories/sub-criteria that were actually verified — so it reflects the strength of what COULD be confirmed, not a penalty for what couldn't be found.
• The "Data Completeness" column (far right of the Scorecard) shows what % of the 17 total sub-criteria were backed by a real source, for THIS vendor. A low Grand Total paired with low Data Completeness means "we don't know enough yet" — treat this differently from a low Grand Total paired with high Data Completeness, which means "we checked thoroughly and it genuinely scored poorly."
• Practical use: before excluding or deprioritizing a vendor based on a low Grand Total, check its Data Completeness — a vendor at 40-50% completeness is a candidate for further manual research/direct outreach, not necessarily a bad fit.
